# RackFlow

AVL system design for the Mac.

- **Wiring diagrams** with live connection validation, patching multiples and colour-coded system groups
- **Rack elevations** with snapping and weight, power and heat analytics
- **Building power**: circuits, breakers and load roll-up from the wall to every device
- **Patch bay labels** generated from your wiring, laid out PatchCAD-style and printed at exact scale
- **Schedule**: every cable and device as an editable spreadsheet, with bulk renumbering and CSV export
- **A gear library** of about 2,300 products from 350+ manufacturers, plus builders for custom gear, wall plates and floor boxes

## Download

**[⬇︎ Download the latest RackFlow for Mac](https://github.com/creativedamage/RackFlow/releases/latest)**

On the release page, pick the DMG for your Mac:

| Your Mac | File |
|---|---|
| Apple Silicon (M1, M2, M3, M4…) | `RackFlow-x.y.z-arm64.dmg` |
| Intel | `RackFlow-x.y.z-x64.dmg` |

(Not sure which? Apple menu → **About This Mac**: "Chip: Apple M…" means Apple Silicon.)

## Install

1. Open the DMG and drag **RackFlow** into **Applications**.
2. Open RackFlow from Applications. The first time, macOS says it can't check the developer, because the app isn't notarized by Apple yet.
   Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway** next to RackFlow. You only need to do this once.
3. Optional: **File → Open…** `examples/Demo Conference Room.rackflow` from this repository to explore a finished project.

## Updates

RackFlow keeps itself up to date. Shortly after it opens, and every few hours while it runs, it checks this repository for a newer release. If there is one, it downloads it in the background and a **Restart to Update** button appears in the toolbar:

- **Restart Now** installs it and reopens RackFlow on the new version (you're asked to save first if you have unsaved changes).
- **Later**: the update installs when you next quit, so the next launch is current.
- **RackFlow → Check for Updates…** checks straight away.

Keep RackFlow in **Applications** so it can update itself. If it can't write there, it offers the new DMG instead.

## Using it

| Do this | How |
|---|---|
| Add gear | Drag from the left library onto the diagram, or **double-click** an item in the Library. Clicked items land in the middle of the current view (on the Wiring Diagram even when you're on another page); ⌘Z undoes. |
| Wire | Drag from a port dot. Valid targets ring **green**, ones that work with caveats ring **amber**, and invalid ones dim and refuse the drop. The bottom hint says why. |
| Pan / zoom | Two-finger scroll pans, pinch zooms (⌘-scroll with a mouse). Drag on empty canvas to box-select. |
| Patch multiples | **Shift-click** ports to pick them (numbered badges show the order; they can be on different devices). Then drag from any picked port to the first destination. The rest land on the following ports of that device, skipping ports that are in use or incompatible. One ⌘Z undoes the batch, and Esc clears the picks. |
| Rack | ⌘2. Drag devices from "Not yet racked" into a rack; the ghost shows exactly where it will snap. Double-click a device to flip front/rear. |
| Rack panels | Drag **Blank**, **Logo**, **Brush**, **Vent**, **Lacing bar**, **Shelf** or **Drawer** panels from the Panels bar into a rack. Click one to change its height, type or label. Logo blanks take your own image (**Upload Image…**, PNG or SVG) on a black, white or silver panel. **Blanks ▾** fills the gaps between devices (or all empty space) with blank panels. |
| Print rack elevations | **Print / Export…** on the Rack page draws CAD-style elevations: rack names above, ears and screws, tag on the left, maker / model / type in the middle, blank, brush, vent and logo panels drawn and labelled. Choose sheet size, front / rear / both, which racks, and a title block (project, client, designer, revision, date, sheet, your logo). PDF, SVG or print. |
| Tidy Up | **✨ Tidy Up** (⌥⌘L) re-arranges the diagram by signal flow: sources on the left, destinations on the right, groups kept together, separate systems stacked, network switches and PDUs in a row underneath. Every wire gets its own lane so parallel runs don't stack or cross. With several devices selected it tidies just those. **Auto-tidy** does it whenever you add a device or cable. ⌘Z undoes it. |
| Wire style | **All wires** draws every run. **Smart** turns long or tangled runs into short labelled stubs at each end ("V-0012 ▸ VSW-001 · In 3"), like a CAD drawing. **Stubs** does that for every wire. When several stubs on one side of a device go to the **same device**, they're bundled: each keeps its cable number and port, and a bracket leads to one extra stub naming the device ("DSP-01 · 12×8 Dante DSP ×2") – on screen and in print. Port rows are 24 px apart (wires between neighbouring ports have room), and cable-number labels are placed so they never overlap – parallel runs get a neat column of labels. Select a cable to see it in full. |
| Notes & callouts | **＋ Note ▾** on the diagram places a **Callout** (box with an arrow you aim by dragging its dot), a **Note**, or a **Revision Note** (Δ revision cloud) wherever you want. Double-click to type, drag to move, drag the corners to resize, Delete removes. Notes belong to the page they were placed on and print on that sheet. |
| Revisions | In any Print dialog, **Revisions** keeps the history (Rev, Date, Description, By). The latest six print in a revision block above the title block, and the newest revision fills the title block's REV box. |
| Print wiring diagrams | **Print…** on the diagram makes a CAD-style drawing: device blocks with tag, maker, model and type, a connector badge on every port (BNC, HDMI, RJ45…), the cable number and FROM-TO tags at both ends of every wire, dashed room zones and off-sheet arrows for long runs. Use one sheet for everything, one per group or one per room. Wires that leave a sheet name the device, port and sheet they go to. Same title block, PDF, SVG or print. In the preview, zoom with the slider, + / − or ⌘-scroll / pinch, and drag the sheet to move around. |
| Patch labels | ⌘3 opens a PatchCAD-style editor. Add bays from the **Templates** panel. Click or drag to select cells, type to edit (Tab for the next cell), **⌘B/I/U** to format, **⌘G** to merge cells into a group title, **123 / ABC / Σ Fill** to number cells, and the ↕ ↓ ‖ buttons (or clicking a symbol) to set normalling. Colours apply per cell or per strip. Copy and paste works with Excel and Numbers. **⟳ From wiring** fills untyped cells from the cables on each bay's rear ports. **Print / Export…** shows a 1:1 preview for PDF, SVG or printing. |
| Library | The left **Library** is one list for everything. Search, then narrow it with the dropdowns: **Show** (All devices, In this project, Patch bays & panels, Wall plates/boxes & panels, ★ My custom & edited), **Maker**, **Category** and **Signal**. Each row has a coloured signal dot and "Maker · Category"; the list loads more as you scroll. Double-click or drag to add. **In this project** lists what's placed (tag, model, room, page) – click one to jump to it. **＋ New ▾** makes a Custom Device, Find Device, Wall Plate, Floor Box, Rack Connector Panel, Patch Bay Template, LED Wall or Rack. |
| Manufacturers | Pick a maker in the Library's **Maker** dropdown to see only their gear (with counts). New makers are added from the device editor's Manufacturer field. |
| Building power | Search **receptacle** (or open the **Building Power** category) and drop in a wall/floor outlet, twist-lock (L5-20/30, L6, L14, L21), Cam-Lok company switch or generator. Set its **panel, circuit and breaker** in the Inspector. Wire a PDU's or device's plug to it ("Plugged in directly" when the cord is attached). The Inspector totals the load of everything downstream, through PDUs, distros and UPSs, against the breaker and its 80% continuous limit. Overloaded circuits are flagged, and every device shows which circuit it's fed from. |
| Find a device | **🔍 Find Device…** (⇧⌘F) searches by brand, model, part number or type, and forgives typos. If the device isn't in the library, build it from the manufacturer's spec sheet: paste the **Connections** section, give it the tech-specs page address, or (with your own Anthropic API key) let **AI lookup** find the specs online. Every port is listed for review: change the quantity, label, connector, direction or signal, delete extras, add missing ones, tick **These ports are correct**, then **Save & Add to Diagram**. **Open in Full Editor…** gives you everything else. |
| Edit devices | Double-click a device on the diagram, right-click it, press ⌘E, or select it and click **Edit Device…**. **Save to This Device Only** changes just that one (it's badged *Edited*). **Save to Library & Update All** changes the product and every placed copy. Built-in and manufacturer products can be edited too: right-click them (or hover and click **Edit**) in the Library. Your version replaces the built-in one and is badged *Edited*, and **Revert** brings back the original. |
| Patch bays | On the Patch Labels page, click **+ Add Patch Bay** and then **Add** on any template, or use **Add** / double-click in the Library with **Show: Patch bays & panels**. Every bay is also placed on the Wiring Diagram. Patch bay jacks are passive, so any row can take a source or a destination, and bay-to-bay tie lines work. A signal above the bay's rating (e.g. 12G into a 3G bay) is a warning, not a block. |
| Network patch panels | The Library (Show: Patch bays & panels) has RJ45 panels: Leviton eXtreme Cat6 / Cat6A and GigaMax Cat5e (24-port 1RU and 48-port 2RU as 2×24), Leviton QuickPort and Panduit Mini-Com keystone panels, and generic punch-down, feed-through (incl. 48-port high-density) and keystone panels, plus fiber LC and XLR feed-through panels. These are wired on **both sides**: the front jack (patch cord to the switch) on the left of the card and the rear termination (punch-down, coupler or keystone) on the right, numbered straight through (Port 1–48). **Layout…** on a bay (or **Change Layout…** in the Inspector) changes rows (**+ Add 1RU Row**), sockets per row, termination (punch-down, feed-through, keystone punch-down, keystone coupler), category (Cat5e / Cat6 / Cat6A / Cat8, shielded) and applies it to that bay, keeping its labels. Cables pick the panel's category automatically (patch cords at the front, horizontal punch-down cable at the rear), you can change the cable type in the Inspector or Schedule, and a lower-category cable on a higher-rated panel is flagged. |
| Plate designer | **＋ New ▾ → Wall Box / Wall Plate / Floor Box / Rack Connector Panel**, or **Design** on any plate, opens a designer like RedCo's: choose a **wall box** (surface-mount back box with a panel – UCP 3.5" × 3.5", 6" × 6", 8" × 8", 10" × 10", 12" × 12", 8.5" × 5.75", 8.5" × 7.5", 14.5" × 7.5", 14.5" × 10.5", 17" × 7.5", 17" × 10.5", each portrait or landscape, or custom; panel screws at the corners, box walls shown dashed, 4" deep), 1–4 gang, Decora or midsize gang plates, 2–8 compartment floor boxes, poke-thrus, stage pockets, wall boxes, 1–4RU rack panels or a custom size, then click or drag connectors onto the face – XLR, speakON, etherCON, opticalCON, BNC, HDMI, USB, DisplayPort, TRS, LC fiber (Neutrik D-series 26 × 31 mm), RJ45 / RCA / 3.5 mm keystones, D-sub, Euroblock, binding posts, duplex 5-15R / 5-20R (Decora size), L5-30, powerCON TRUE1, Schuko. Drawings are outline-only line art at real size. Drag to move with a snap grid (0.5–10 mm), Shift-click or drag a box to select several, **align** (left / centre / right / top / middle / bottom), **distribute** with equal gaps, centre on the face, nudge with the arrows, ⌘D duplicates, ⌫ deletes, ⌘Z undoes. Label each jack (the label is printed on the plate and becomes the port name) and add **text anywhere**. Each jack is a port: room side on the left of the diagram card, tie line on the right; removing one keeps the others' cables. The face drawing shows at the top of the device on the wiring diagram and on printed drawings. |
| Custom gear | **＋ New ▾ → Custom Device…**, or right-click a library item and choose **Duplicate as Custom**. Delete one with right-click → **Delete Product…** or **Delete Product…** in its editor. **Show: ★ My custom & edited** lists only yours. The Manufacturer field searches the directory. If you type a maker that isn't listed, choose **+ Add as a new manufacturer** and it's saved to your list along with the product. |
| Custom panels | **Patch Bays → + New Patch Bay Template**, or **New… / Customize…** in the Templates panel, opens a PatchCAD-style editor. *The Important Stuff* tab sets rows, sockets per row, lines per strip, Mono Spaced or Paired layout (and pair spacing), and strip padding (left/right/top/bottom). It also sets the distance from strip to socket, the socket diameter and the strip height. Below that is a width for **every cell**, with **Add Gap at Cursor**, **Add Gap in the Middle**, **Delete Gap** and **Repeat Till End**, **Set all to** and **Fit total to**, and a live total label width and preview. The *Fonts* tab sets default label styling and *Notes* holds part numbers. **Save**, **Save As…**, **Delete Template…** and **Share Template…** (a .rftemplate.json, opened with **Import…** in the Templates panel). A caliper "Measure a physical panel" helper is still there. |
| Schedule (spreadsheet) | **Schedule** (⌘4) shows every cable and every device as a spreadsheet. Click, Shift-click or drag to select. Type or press Enter to edit and Tab to move. Copy and paste blocks to and from Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets, ⌘D fills down and Delete clears. Click a header to sort, and filter by text or system. In Cables you can edit Label, Cable Type (dropdown of cables that fit), Length and Notes. In Devices you can edit Tag, Room and Notes. **Renumber…** relabels cables per system (video, audio, network, power…) with your own prefix, start number, digit count and separator. It can number in source, destination, label or drawing order, for all, selected or filtered rows, and keeps every label unique. **Export CSV** on both tabs. Every bulk change is one undo. |
| Network sheet | **Schedule → Network** lists every network interface (Ethernet, Dante primary / secondary, NDI, AVB…) with **IP Address**, **Device ID**, Interface, **Manufacturer**, **Model**, **Location**, **Switch**, **Switch Port**, **VLAN**, Cable and **Notes**. Switch and port are traced from the wiring, through wall plates and patch panels; where a device isn't cabled yet you can type them in (shown in italics). Invalid or duplicate IPs are flagged in red. **Assign IPs…** numbers the shown (or selected) rows from a starting address, skipping ones already in use. Paste to and from Excel / Google Sheets, and **Export CSV**. |
| Title blocks | **Edit Title Block…** in any Print dialog designs the strip along the bottom of every sheet: add, remove and reorder boxes, choose what each shows (project, client, location, designer, drawing, revision, date, sheet number, or fixed text such as your company name and address), set widths, one or two rows, big text, the block's height, and a **logo** (left or right, any width). **Use for This Project** saves it – logo included – in the project, so it's there next time. **Save as Template** keeps it for every project; load saved templates from **Start from**. The Logo row in the print dialog uploads / replaces the title-block logo directly. |
| Numbering standards | **Edit → Numbering Standards…** (or the button on the Schedule) sets how tags and cable labels are built. **Device tags**: per category prefix, separator, digits and start (PB-001, DSP-01, AV-PJ-1…). **Cable labels**: the same per system (A-0001, V.100…). New devices and cables follow it; **Save & Renumber Devices / Cables** applies it to everything already drawn (top-to-bottom, left-to-right). **Save as Standard** stores it by name to load in any project. Schedule → Renumber… starts from the standard too. |
| Sheet numbers | **# Sheet Numbers** at the right of the page tabs (or right-click a page tab, or **Sheet Numbers…** in Print…) numbers your wiring pages. Turn on **Number pages automatically** and set a prefix, start and step (AV + 101 → AV101, AV102…), and/or type a custom number for any page or signal view (AV201, E-001…). Numbers show on the tabs, in the title block's SHEET box and in off-sheet arrows ("SHT AV102") and on-screen off-page flags. Duplicates are flagged. For the other drawings (racks, patch labels, LED maps), the print dialog's **Drawing no.** counts up per sheet: AV501 → AV501, AV502… |
| Signal views | A view is an extra page that shows devices from your diagram with **only the ports you choose** – e.g. *Audio Tie Lines* with just the wall plates, floor boxes and patch bays and their audio ports – while the main diagram keeps every port. Click **＋ View**, pick the signals (a port matches when its main signal is that type) and tick the devices (**All matching**, **Plates, boxes & patch bays**, or one by one). Click a device's **“n of m ports ▸”** to tick exactly which of its ports show; in the view, right-click a port → **Hide in This View**, **Hide Unwired Ports**, or **Choose Ports…**. **Only show ports that are wired** keeps big patch bays short. Click a device in a view for **S / M / L**: small shows just the plate drawing (wires still attach), medium the drawing and ports, large a big drawing. A view has its own layout (✨ Tidy Up arranges just the view). Wires to devices outside the view end in flags. Delete only takes a device off the view. Views print as their own sheets. |
| LED walls | **LED Walls** (⌘5), or the **LED wall** card in the library. **＋ New Wall** picks a panel from ROE Visual, Absen, INFiLED, Unilumin, Leyard, Aoto, Gloshine and generic sizes (search by maker, model or pitch), or **＋ Custom Panel** for your own (pitch, cabinet size, pixels, weight, max / typical watts, connectors – saved in the project). Set columns × rows; **Edit shape** lets you click panels out for irregular walls. **Data Flow** and **Power Flow** are separate maps: each processor port / circuit gets its own colour, panels are numbered in order (P1.1, P1.2… / C1.1…), arrows show the run, and a dashed line with a **PORT n / CIRCUIT n** tag marks the home run. Choose the home-run corner, columns or rows, snake or raster, the processor (NovaStar, Brompton, Colorlight, Megapixel or your own pixels per port), frame rate and bit depth, voltage and circuit size; panels per port / circuit are calculated (or set your own). Totals show resolution, aspect, size, weight, power and amps. **Add to Wiring Diagram** places the wall as a device with one Data input per port and one Power input per circuit, kept in step as you edit. **Print / Export…** draws data and power sheets with a stats box and title block. Built-in panel specs are nominal; check them against the manufacturer's sheet. |
| Export everything | **File → Export Everything…** (⇧⌘E) saves a new folder with every drawing as a PDF (wiring diagram – one sheet per page – rack elevations front and rear, patch bay labels, LED wall maps) and every schedule as a CSV (cables, equipment, network / IP). Tick what you want and pick the sheet size. |
| Open Recent | **File → Open Recent** lists your last twelve projects; **Clear Menu** empties it. |
| Light / dark mode | The ◐ / ☀ / ☾ button in the toolbar switches between **System** (follows macOS), **Light** and **Dark**; or use **View → Appearance**. RackFlow remembers your choice. Printed drawings are always black on white. |
| Undo / redo | ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z or the toolbar arrows; hover them to see what will be undone. In a text field you're typing in, ⌘Z undoes the typing; once you've stopped editing it undoes the document. Bulk changes (renumber, patch multiples, grouping, adding gear) show a notification with an **Undo** button. |
| Pages (sheets) | Split a big diagram into sheets. **＋ Page** in the tab bar above the diagram makes a new page (the first time, everything already drawn goes on Page 1 so nothing goes missing). Move devices to another page by **dragging them onto its tab**, or right-click → **Move to Page ▸ …** / **Move to New Page** / **Remove from Page** (works on the whole selection). Moved devices land in open space on that page, and every wire left behind becomes an off-page flag automatically. You can also select devices and press **⌘G**, or use **Pages by system** in the Pages panel. **All pages** shows everything. On a page, wires to devices on other pages show as labelled flags on the port ("V-0015 ▸ CNV-01 · HDMI In (Video)"), and anything you add goes on that page. Printing makes **one sheet per page**, named after the page, with off-sheet arrows that give the other page's sheet number. Rename a page by double-clicking it in the Pages panel; × deletes the page (its devices stay, unassigned). |
| Locations | A device's **Room** now shows on its card under the tag. With several devices selected, the Inspector sets the room for all of them at once. |
| Design check | The toolbar pill shows error and warning counts. Click it for a list you can filter (All, Errors, Warnings, Notes); click an issue to jump to it. The right-hand panel only opens when something is selected, and you can drag its left edge to make it wider (double-click the edge to reset). |

Your custom products, manufacturers and templates are kept in `~/Library/Application Support/RackFlow/`, so updating the app never touches them.

## Data caveats

- **Catalog specs are approximate.** Model names and I/O follow each manufacturer's published range. Weight, depth and power are approximate, and some I/O is simplified (for example, card-slot frames list their fixed ports only). Every catalog product shows a "check the spec sheet" note in the Inspector. Before ordering or doing load calculations, **Duplicate** the product, correct it from the spec sheet and tick the "I've checked these specs" box. Product ranges change, so a current model may be missing or a listed one discontinued.
- **Patch bay templates** from Bittree, Neutrik, ADC, Canare and Redco use nominal strip dimensions and are flagged in the app. Verify them against the manufacturer's label template before printing a full run, or measure the real panel in the Template Builder.
